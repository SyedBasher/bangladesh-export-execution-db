The problem

Most trade databases stop too early.

Market size, growth, tariffs and export potential are useful. They do not answer the questions that determine whether an opportunity is executable.

Common layer

Where is the opportunity?

Market size, growth, trade shares, tariffs, product relatedness and competitor structure.

BEOED layer

Can Bangladesh enter and persist?

Historical entry transitions, survival, destination familiarity, product experience and observable market structure.

Commissioned layer

Who can execute—and what blocks them?

Candidate firm capability, certification and input constraints, scenario analysis, financing needs and domestic value capture.

Architecture

One database, four evidence layers.

Every layer carries provenance and an explicit distinction between observed, derived, model-estimated and unknown information.

Method

Designed to be auditable.

Manufactured variables are useful only if a user can reconstruct what went into them and distinguish prediction from causal evidence.

01

Versioned inputs

Core trade statistics are pinned to a single CEPII BACI vintage rather than mixing revisions across years.

02

Out-of-time validation

Entry and survival models hold later cohorts out rather than reporting in-sample fit as predictive evidence.

03

Evidence labels

Observed, derived, model-estimated and unknown fields remain distinguishable throughout the database.

04

Interpretation limits

Scores are diagnostics, not causal estimates, guarantees, credit ratings or investment recommendations.
